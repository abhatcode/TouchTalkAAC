Why I made it

I started TouchTalk after seeing how hard it was for people with disabilities, including my brother, to communicate. The AAC apps I found cost money, needed an account, or were clunky to set up.

I tested it with someone who has cerebral palsy, and their feedback decided a lot of how it works: the button sizes, how the categories are grouped, and keeping the Help button reachable on every screen. My little brother helps me test it too.

The developer, a high school junior

Who uses it

AAC stands for augmentative and alternative communication: any tool that helps a person communicate when speech is hard.

  • Cerebral palsyLarge buttons, hold to select, and ignore repeat taps for limited hand control.
  • ALS and MNDWorks with a keyboard or switch that sends key presses.
  • AutismWords stay in the same place, so the layout can be learned.
  • AphasiaPictures and ready-made phrases reduce the need to find each word.
  • Selective mutismA way to answer when speaking out loud is not possible.
  • After surgery or intubationShort-term communication in hospital or at home.

What it does

  • Speaks out loud

    Each word can be spoken as you tap it, or build a full sentence and press Speak. Change the voice, speed, and pitch.

  • Core word board

    60 common words in fixed places, colour coded by word type with the Fitzgerald Key used in many AAC systems.

  • Quick phrases

    Whole sentences such as "I need help, please" in one tap. Add your own.

  • Help button

    Always at the top of the screen. One press says "Help. I need assistance now."

  • Your own words and voice

    Add categories and words, use real photos, and record a family member's voice for names.

  • Settings for motor needs

    Hold to select, ignore repeat taps, and four button sizes. Nothing on screen flashes.

  • Type to speak

    For people who can spell, with next-word suggestions that learn from what you say.

  • Works offline

    Install it to the home screen and it keeps working without internet.

  • Backup to a file

    Save your words and settings to a file and open it on a new device.

Questions

Does TouchTalk collect any data?

No. There are no analytics, no tracking, and no server. Words, phrases, recordings, and settings are stored in the browser on your device and are never sent anywhere.

Does it work without internet?

Yes, after the first visit. Speech uses the voice built into your device, so it does not need a connection either.

Which languages does it speak?

Any language your device has a voice for. The app's menus are in English. You can add words in any language, or record your own voice for them.

Can I add my own words and pictures?

Yes. In the app, open Edit. You can add categories, words, and phrases, give each one a photo or an icon, and record a voice for it.

Does it work with switches or eye tracking?

Every button can be reached with the Tab key, arrow keys move around the word board, and the selected button has a thick yellow and black outline. Switches and eye-gaze systems that send key presses or mouse clicks will work. Automatic switch scanning is planned.

Is this a medical device?

No. TouchTalk is a communication aid. It is not certified by the FDA or any other regulator and does not replace an assessment by a speech-language pathologist.

Why is it free?

Dedicated AAC devices can cost thousands of dollars. TouchTalk is a personal project with no ads and nothing to sell.
